Today's sports wearables integrate artificial intelligence, advanced recovery metrics, and detailed biometrics—such as the Garmin Forerunner series and the Galaxy Ring—helping athletes optimize their training.

Jump Trackers

Small wearable sensors, usually positioned on the lower back, use accelerometers and gyroscopes to record the athletes movements. They measure the height and number of jumps performed during training, allowing the workload to be monitored and possible joint overload to be identified. This technology is already used in professional volleyball, mainly for monitoring and analyzing athletes performance.

-

Whoop

A wearable device used by athletes to monitor recovery and key physiological indicators. The wristbands or straps, worn on the wrist, forearm, or chest, record heart rate, heart rate variability (HRV), sleep quality, and rest time. The technology is already used by professional athletes to monitor recovery and help manage training load.

-

Smart Textiles

Clothing with integrated sensors allows for more detailed body monitoring during sports activities. These sensors can record biometric data, sweat rates, and biomechanical parameters during movement, providing information about the athlete’s effort and physical condition. The technology is still under development and being tested in different sports applications.
